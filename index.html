Climate Change in Texas

What the data reveals about rising temperatures, changing rainfall, and extreme weather.

Using scientific research and climate data to show how Texas is already being affected.

↓

Introduction

Climate change is often discussed as a global issue, but its effects are becoming increasingly visible at the regional level. In Texas, rising temperatures, shifting precipitation patterns, and more frequent extreme weather events are already impacting communities, ecosystems, and infrastructure.

This project uses long-term climate datasets and peer-reviewed scientific studies to examine how climate patterns in Texas have changed over time. By analyzing temperature trends, precipitation variability, and extreme weather conditions, this site presents clear, data-driven evidence that climate change is not a distant problem, it is happening now.

Thesis Statement

Texas is already experiencing measurable effects of climate change, including rising temperatures, increased variability in precipitation, worsening drought conditions, and more frequent extreme weather events.

Key Findings

Rising Temperatures

Texas has experienced a long-term increase in average temperatures, with more frequent and intense heat events.

Changing Rainfall Patterns

Precipitation is becoming more variable, with both heavier rainfall events and longer dry periods.

Increasing Drought Risk

Higher temperatures and shifting rainfall patterns are contributing to more severe drought conditions.

More Extreme Weather

Climate change is increasing the likelihood and intensity of extreme weather events such as heatwaves and heavy rainfall.

Why This Matters

Climate change affects more than just temperature, it influences water availability, agriculture, public health, and infrastructure. In a state like Texas, where weather plays a critical role in daily life and economic activity, even small shifts in climate patterns can have significant consequences.

Understanding these trends is important not only for scientists and policymakers, but also for everyday residents who are directly impacted by changing environmental conditions.

Why Focus on Texas?

While climate change is a global issue, its impacts vary by region. Texas presents a unique and important case study due to its size, climate diversity, and vulnerability to extreme weather.
