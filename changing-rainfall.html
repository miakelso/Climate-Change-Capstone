Changing Rainfall

Rainfall in Texas is becoming less predictable, with increasing swings between wet and dry conditions.

The Pattern is Changing

Climate change is not simply making Texas wetter or drier. Instead, it is increasing variability in precipitation. This means that rainfall patterns are becoming less predictable, with larger swings between extremely wet years and extremely dry years.

What the Data Shows

The data shows that precipitation in Texas fluctuates significantly from year to year. Some years experience much higher rainfall than average, while others are much drier. There is no consistent upward or downward trend. Instead, the pattern is irregular and highly variable.

This variability is the key takeaway. Rather than a steady change, Texas is experiencing more unpredictable rainfall patterns, which makes planning and resource management more difficult.

Increasing Extremes

Risser et al. (2023) Precipitation has “large internal variability,” making trends difficult to detect while extreme events become more important.

Scientific research supports what the data shows. According to Risser et al., precipitation patterns naturally contain a high level of variability, but climate change is increasing the importance of extreme events. This means that even if average rainfall does not change dramatically, the intensity and frequency of extreme rainfall events can still increase.

Kunkel et al. (2013) The region experiences highly variable precipitation, including periods of both severe drought and heavy rainfall.

In Texas and the broader Great Plains, this variability is already a defining characteristic of the climate. Climate change is amplifying these extremes, making both flooding and drought more likely.

Why This Matters

Flooding

Heavier rainfall events increase the risk of flash flooding and infrastructure damage.

Water Management

Unpredictable rainfall makes it harder to manage reservoirs and water supply.

Infrastructure

Stormwater systems and drainage infrastructure are strained by extreme rainfall events.

Agriculture

Farmers must deal with both drought conditions and sudden heavy rainfall.

Source: NOAA National Centers for Environmental Information, PRISM Climate Group, Risser et al. (2023), and Kunkel et al. (2013).

Key Concept: Climate change does not simply increase or decrease rainfall. It increases variability, leading to more extreme swings between wet and dry conditions.
